What deseasonalized means here. For each basin, the code takes the average of every January in the record and removes it from every January. It repeats that for each month of the year. What's left in each month is the part its own time of year fails to explain. This matters for annual figures, because 2018 holds only June to December and 2026 only January to March. Averaging raw months would put one year's summer beside another year's winter. Those two years wear a dashed outline in the year buttons.

The three frames. Level gives an anomaly, so zero means that basin's own record mean rather than any absolute amount of water. Change from last year takes the difference between two annual means. No such difference exists for 2018, the first year, which leaves the map colorless on that frame. First year to last runs the whole record end to end, the 2026 annual mean minus the 2018 one, and it holds still as the animation runs.

Read the endpoint difference with care. It rests on the 5 solved months of 2018 and the 3 of 2026, so 8 of the 92 solutions in the record. It throws away everything between them. The fitted rate on the other page uses all 92, so the two answers can disagree. Where they do, a wet or dry patch at one end has usually pushed the endpoint difference around. The tooltip and the table show both numbers together, which keeps the disagreement visible rather than hidden.

Limits to know. An annual mean from 7 to 12 monthly solutions still holds the measurement error of those solutions. No significance test runs on a single year, so read the frames as a sequence rather than as year-by-year verdicts. A Goddard mascon spans about 12,400 km², so small basins share their signal with their neighbors.

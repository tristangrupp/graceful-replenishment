Does any of it agree with a well?

The downscaling test used no outside data, so it could only detect failure. This page brings in the one reference that can say whether a value is right: measured groundwater levels. Two questions are asked of every well. Does a downscaled product track it better than the coarse solution behind it? And does gravimetry of any resolution tell you something about the well that precipitation, soil moisture and snow do not already?

What this page can speak for

The open well record is not global. It is North America, with Mexico, France, Germany and Scandinavia, some of Brazil, and New Zealand. Asia is almost absent. The compilation behind the bulk of it covers more than 40 countries, but only the part its database managers permitted to be reposted is available, and that part leaves out North India, the North China Plain, Iran and the Arabian Peninsula. Those are the places where the downscaling test found the most, and this page cannot say anything about them.

Mexico comes from a second source, CONAGUA's own national piezometric network, added because it puts a semi-arid irrigated aquifer system outside the United States into the comparison. The two sources do not overlap: the nearest Mexican well to any well in the first source is 668 metres away, and that is checked rather than assumed.

Within the region it does cover, the well network is denser than the measurement. Tens of thousands of wells sit inside a few hundred GRACE mascons, and a mascon is one gravimetric observation. Every test on this page therefore counts mascons rather than wells. Counting wells would turn a few hundred facts into tens of thousands of them and would make almost any difference look certain.

How a well is compared

Annual depth to water is negated once, so that up means more water, and turned into an anomaly against the well's own mean. Each product's total water storage is read at the cell containing the well and the land surface term, GLDAS soil moisture, snow and canopy, is subtracted at that product's own cell size. What remains is compared to the well.

Groundwater level is a head, not a storage. Converting one to the other needs a specific yield, which is not known per well and would be the largest free parameter in the comparison. Nothing here converts. Every score is a correlation or an out of sample R squared on standardized series, and both are unchanged by any positive scale factor.

What it still cannot do

A well measures a head at a point in one aquifer. A GRACE cell measures a mass change over thousands of square kilometers through the whole column. They are not the same quantity, and a poor correlation can mean the product is wrong, the well is unrepresentative, or a confined aquifer is responding to pressure rather than storage. The comparison is evidence, not adjudication.

The ablation is the stronger of the two tests here, because it asks what a product adds rather than how well it agrees. It still rests on a linear model of annual values, and a product could hold information a linear model cannot use.

How fast each basin is gaining or losing water

Colour is a rate: the slope of one line fitted through all 92 monthly solutions at once, in millimetres of equivalent water height per year. A basin at −20 mm/yr lost about 20 mm of stored water in an average year of this record. It is not the difference between 2018 and 2026, and it is not change within a year.       How to read it. Total water storage is everything GRACE senses: groundwater, soil moisture, snow, surface water and ice. The groundwater estimate subtracts soil moisture, snow water equivalent and canopy storage modelled by GLDAS, averaged over the NOAH, VIC and CLSM land-surface models, so it is a difference between a measurement and a model ensemble rather than a measurement. Basins more than a fifth glacier or ice sheet are withheld from it, because subtracting modelled snowpack from ice-sheet mass loss does not give groundwater.

      What the marks mean. Hatching marks a basin whose rate cannot be separated from zero at p < 0.05 after correcting for month-to-month autocorrelation. The dashed line on the chart is that fitted rate, drawn through the record mean; the faint dotted line is the area-weighted global land mean, for scale.

      Limits to know. The window is short for a rate and about half of all basins are hatched. GSFC mascons are roughly 12,400 km², so a basin backed by only a few of them is reporting its mascons’ number rather than its own, and that count is in the table. Neighbouring basins share mascons and are not independent measurements. Leakage is shipped by GSFC as an uncertainty rather than a correction, so every magnitude here is a floor.

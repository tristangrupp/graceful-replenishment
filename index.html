How fast each basin is gaining or losing water

Color is a rate. It shows the slope of one line fitted through all 92 monthly solutions from the Gravity Recovery and Climate Experiment (GRACE), in millimeters of water per year. A basin at −20 mm/yr lost about 20 mm of stored water in an average year of this record. That isn't the difference between 2018 and 2026, and it isn't change within a year.       How to read the map

      Total water storage is all the water GRACE detects. That includes groundwater, soil moisture, snow, surface water, and ice.

      The groundwater layer subtracts three modeled terms: soil moisture, snow water equivalent, and canopy storage. They come from the Global Land Data Assimilation System, averaged across its Noah, VIC, and CLSM models. That makes the groundwater layer a measurement minus a model average. Treat it as an estimate, not a reading.

      Some basins are more than one fifth glacier or ice sheet. The groundwater layer leaves those out. Subtracting modeled snow from ice sheet loss doesn't measure groundwater.

      What the marks mean

      • Hatching. The trend isn't distinguishable from zero at p < 0.05, after a correction for month-to-month autocorrelation.
      • Grey fill. No value in this layer.
      • Dashed line. The fitted rate for a selected basin, drawn through its record mean.
      • Dotted line. The global land average, for scale.

      Limits

      • The record is short. It holds 92 monthly solutions, and hatching covers about half the basins.
      • One mascon covers about 12,400 km². A small basin rests on a few mascons, so it reports their signal rather than its own. The table shows how many.
      • Neighboring basins share mascons. They aren't independent measurements.
      • Leakage is an uncertainty, not a correction. Read every value as a lower bound.
      • Glacier cover comes from Natural Earth's 10m glaciated areas. The extremes list can use it to drop basins whose trend is ice loss. It draws coarse polygons, so treat the threshold as approximate.

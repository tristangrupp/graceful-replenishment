How fast each basin is gaining or losing water

Color is a rate. It shows the slope of one line fitted through all 92 monthly solutions from the Gravity Recovery and Climate Experiment (GRACE), in millimeters of water per year. A basin at −20 mm/yr lost about 20 mm of stored water in an average year of this record. That isn't the difference between 2018 and 2026, and it isn't change within a year.       How to read it. Total water storage covers everything GRACE senses: groundwater, soil moisture, snow, surface water, and ice. The groundwater estimate subtracts modelled soil moisture, snow water equivalent, and canopy storage. Those come from the Global Land Data Assimilation System (GLDAS), averaged over three land surface models, namely Noah, Variable Infiltration Capacity (VIC), and the Catchment Land Surface Model (CLSM). That makes it a difference between a measurement and a model ensemble, not a measurement itself. The map withholds any basin more than a fifth glacier or ice sheet, because subtracting modelled snowpack from ice-sheet mass loss doesn't give groundwater.

      What the marks mean. Hatching marks a basin whose rate the test can't separate from zero at p < 0.05, after it corrects for month-to-month autocorrelation. The dashed line on the chart draws that fitted rate through the record mean. The faint dotted line gives the area-weighted global land mean, for scale.

      Limits to know. The window runs short for a rate, and hatching covers about half the basins. A Goddard mascon spans about 12,400 km². A basin backed by only a few of them reports its mascons’ number rather than its own, and the table gives that count. Neighboring basins share mascons, so they aren't independent measurements. Goddard ships leakage as an uncertainty rather than a correction, which makes every value here a floor.

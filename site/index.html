How fast each basin is gaining or losing water

Color is a rate. It shows the slope of one line fitted through all 92 monthly solutions from the Gravity Recovery and Climate Experiment (GRACE), in millimeters of water per year. A basin at −20 mm/yr lost about 20 mm of stored water in an average year of this record. That isn't the difference between 2018 and 2026, and it isn't change within a year. For those, see Year by year.

How it is built

Mass balance. Soil moisture, snow water equivalent and canopy storage subtracted from total water storage.

Three models, not one. Those terms averaged across Noah, VIC and CLSM, so model bias is a spread rather than one choice.

Season fitted out. Annual and semi-annual harmonics fitted alongside the slope, so a rate is a trend.

Autocorrelation discounted. Significance uses an effective sample size, so p < 0.05 is not manufactured by month-to-month correlation.

What it cannot do

A footprint, not a point. One mascon covers about 12,400 km². A small basin reports the mascons under it; the count is in the table.

Leakage is uncertainty. Left uncorrected and stated. Read every magnitude as a lower bound.

Ice excluded. Basins over one fifth glacier or ice sheet are withheld from the groundwater layer.

      How to read the map

      Total water storage is all the water GRACE detects. That includes groundwater, soil moisture, snow, surface water, and ice.

      The groundwater layer subtracts three modeled terms from it, so it is a measurement minus a model average. Treat it as an estimate, not a reading. The method panel at the top of the page states what is subtracted and what is excluded.

      What the marks mean

      • Hatching. The trend isn't distinguishable from zero at p < 0.05, after a correction for month-to-month autocorrelation.
      • Grey fill. No value in this layer.
      • Dashed line. The fitted rate for a selected basin, drawn through its record mean.
      • Dotted line. The global land average, for scale.

      Limits

      • The record is short. It holds 92 monthly solutions, and hatching covers about half the basins.
      • Neighboring basins share mascons, so they aren't independent measurements. The table shows how many mascons each basin rests on.
      • Glacier cover comes from Natural Earth's 10m glaciated areas. The extremes list can use it to drop basins whose trend is ice loss. It draws coarse polygons, so treat the threshold as approximate.

      How the number is made, step by step

      1. Read the mascons. GSFC RL06v2.0, solution OBP-ICE6GD, already corrected for glacial isostatic adjustment. Values are stored in centimeters and converted to millimeters. Ocean mascons are dropped; Greenland, Antarctica and the ice caps are kept as terrestrial mass, because a filter on the ice-free land code alone silently drops Greenland. shared/gsfc_grid.py
      2. Subtract the modeled terms. Soil moisture, snow water equivalent and canopy storage from GLDAS 2.1, averaged over Noah, VIC and CLSM, are aggregated onto the native mascons by cos(lat) weight rather than GRACE being interpolated down. Interpolating up invents structure the measurement does not have. Total water storage minus that average is the groundwater layer. 01_global_gws.py
      3. Fit the trend. One least-squares fit per series: slope, annual and semi-annual harmonics together. Significance uses an effective sample size for residual lag-1 autocorrelation, following Dawdy and Matalas (1964). The estimator itself sits in the dark_water.depletion_watchlist.depletion.trend module, outside this repository; both the mascon step and the basin step call it, so a mascon trend and a basin trend are the same fit on different series. 01_global_gws.py, 02_basins.py
      4. Put it on basins. Mascons and basins do not nest, so every terrestrial cell on a 0.1 degree grid inherits the trend of the mascon containing it and is assigned to the polygon it falls inside. A basin is the cos(lat)-weighted mean of its cells. The basin series is built first and the trend fitted on it, so the p-value belongs to the basin. Coverage is tracked, never averaged in as zero: a basin below half coverage is dropped from the groundwater layer, and so is one over one fifth ice. 02_basins.py
      5. Measure the ice. Glacier share per basin from Natural Earth's 10m glaciated areas. GSFC's own ice codes cannot answer this: they name Greenland, Antarctica and a short list of ice caps, leaving Svalbard, Novaya Zemlya, Ellesmere, Iceland and Patagonia as ordinary land, and those are exactly the basins whose trend is glacier loss. 08_glacier_fraction.py
      6. Check it against a second code path. The regional Arabia run subset GLDAS through OPeNDAP and averaged cells inside each mascon box; this pipeline downloads whole granules and assigns cells by search. Same mascons, same months, compared. 04_crosscheck.py
      7. Export what this page reads. Basins simplified for drawing, trends, p-values, mascon counts, coverage and glacier share, for levels 3 and 4 and for the aquifer units. 06_export_viz.py

      The data

      • GSFC global mascons RL06v2.0, solution OBP-ICE6GD, about 12,400 km² per mascon, 2002-04 to 2026-03. The window here starts at the first GRACE-FO month and ends where this release stops. earth.gsfc.nasa.gov/geo/data/grace-mascons.
      • GLDAS 2.1, Noah 0.25 degrees, VIC 1.0 degrees and CLSM 1.0 degrees, monthly. disc.gsfc.nasa.gov, GLDAS 2.1.
      • HydroSHEDS HydroBASINS v1c, levels 3 and 4, all nine regions. hydrosheds.org/products/hydrobasins.
      • WHYMAP Groundwater Resources of the World v1, BGR and UNESCO, 1:25,000,000, the aquifer unit. whymap.org, Groundwater Resources. 30_aquifer_units.py
      • Natural Earth 10m glaciated areas, public domain, generalised. naturalearthdata.com, 10m physical vectors.

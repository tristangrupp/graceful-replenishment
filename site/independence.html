How many independent measurements is this map?

Neighbouring basins report the same water. Counting them as separate findings overstates the evidence twice over: in how much the map holds, and in how many basins pass a significance test.

The rules

  • p < 0.05. One test per basin, nothing adjusted. On 1,283 basins with no real signal, about 64 would pass by luck.
  • Benjamini-Hochberg. Holds the false discovery rate, the share of wrong calls among the basins you keep, at 5 percent. Valid when tests are independent or positively dependent.
  • Benjamini-Yekutieli. The same walk down the sorted p-values, divided by c(m) = 1 + 1/2 + ... + 1/m, about 7.7 here. Valid under any dependence, which is why this page leads with it.
  • Bonferroni. Allows no false positive anywhere: p < 0.05/m. Right for a drug trial, far too strict for a map.

Method

Moran's I on shared-border weights, 9,999 permutations, with island basins given their four nearest neighbours. The correlogram repeats it in distance bands. The effective count fits rho(d) = exp(-d/a) to those bands and takes n² divided by the sum of rho over every pair, the spatial counterpart of the autocorrelation correction already applied in time.

Two limits. This cannot separate real hydroclimate clustering from the smoothing in a regularized mascon solution. And an effective count describes how much independent information the field holds, not which basins hold it.

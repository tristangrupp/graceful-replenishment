Does downscaled GRACE add anything at level 6?

Two published global products claim to resolve water storage finer than GRACE measures it. GRACE-SeDA runs at 0.5 degrees; Li and Kusche runs at 0.25 degrees. Each one is tested on its own here, against the coarse JPL mascon solution that produced it, on 16,397 HydroBASINS level 6 basins, using no wells and no other outside data. A red flag means there is a specific reason not to treat that basin's result as independently resolved or robust. An unflagged basin does not mean validated: it survived a set of tests designed to identify specific, detectable failure modes, and that is the full claim.

How a basin gets its numbers

Each product is defined on its own grid. Basin boundaries are rasterized once at 0.05 degrees, and each coarser grid inherits its basin overlap from that raster. A basin mean is the area-weighted mean of the cells that overlap the basin: the sum of cell values multiplied by the area of each cell inside the basin, divided by the total area represented. This is a conservative remapping. Nearest-neighbor and bilinear interpolation are not used, because they do not conserve the quantity being aggregated.

If a product has no value for a cell in a given month, that cell is removed from both the numerator and the denominator. A basin is renormalized over the area actually observed rather than treating missing area as zero.

Each product is evaluated on its own terms, against the coarse release it names as its input. The tests do not require the two products to agree, except for the final two, which use their disagreement because that disagreement can be measured without outside observations.

The five tests

testquantitycut
1. Not resolved product cells with centers in the basin; share of basin area in its largest mascon under 2 cells, or over 95%
2. No independent departure variance of (downscaled − coarse) over variance of coarse under 0.05
3. Weather field adjusted R² of the departure on precipitation, soil moisture, snow over 0.8
4. Products disagree correlation of the two series; trend gap over trend magnitude under 0.5, or over 1
5. Ranking unstable shift in percentile rank between the two products over 20 points

1. Not resolved

What it measures. Whether the basin contains enough independent spatial information for the product to support a basin-scale pattern, and whether GRACE sampled the basin at more than a single coarse spatial unit. Two quantities: the number of product cells whose centers fall inside the basin, and the share of the basin's area contained within its largest coarse mascon.

The cut. Fewer than 2 product cells, or more than 95 percent of the basin's area within a single mascon.

What tripping it means. If a basin falls almost entirely within one GRACE mascon, the gravimetric observation available to GRACE is one coarse value. That observation contains no information about how storage varies within the basin. Any finer spatial pattern a downscaled product produces there comes from the model information used in the downscaling, not from additional spatial information measured by GRACE. This is a limitation of spatial resolution, not a question of predictive skill: external validation cannot change the fact that the underlying gravimetric observation did not resolve that pattern.

2. No independent departure

What it measures. How much the downscaled basin series differs from the corresponding coarse GRACE series. The variance of the monthly difference between the two series, divided by the variance of the coarse series.

The cut. Less than 5 percent of the variance of the coarse series.

What tripping it means. The downscaled product is reproducing the coarse series at the basin scale. That may be exactly what the downscaling method is designed to do, and it is not necessarily an error, but it means the finer product has not demonstrated basin-level information beyond the coarse solution.

The comparison needs a reference floor, and two are measured here rather than assumed. Moving one processing center across one release, JPL RL06.1 to RL06.3, costs 0.0005 of the coarse variance in the median basin. Moving between two centers, JPL to GSFC, costs 0.25. A departure smaller than the second is difficult to distinguish from the differences introduced by the choice of GRACE processing center.

3. Weather field

What it measures. Whether the downscaled departure from coarse GRACE is largely explained by hydrometeorological predictor fields rather than by information attributable to the gravimetric observations. The departure series is regressed against basin precipitation at zero to three month lags, accumulated precipitation anomaly, soil moisture, and snow plus canopy.

The cut. Adjusted R² above 0.8.

What tripping it means. A large share of the departure is explained by the same hydrometeorological fields used to characterize the water system. Ranking basins by the departure may then largely amount to ranking them by the spatial and temporal structure of precipitation and soil moisture.

Some relationship is expected, because both products combine GRACE observations with model-derived information. A high R² is not by itself evidence of an error; it indicates how strongly the departure is coupled to the predictor fields. The distinction still matters: a basin at R² 0.95 is behaving differently from one at 0.55. This test flags the fewest basins of the five, and that result is reported rather than hidden.

4. Products disagree

What it measures. Whether the two downscaled products produce similar basin-scale time series and depletion trends. The monthly series are compared by correlation, and the difference between their fitted trends is compared with the magnitude of the trend itself.

The cut. Correlation below 0.5, or a difference between fitted trends larger than the magnitude of the trend.

What tripping it means. The two methods start from the same underlying GRACE observations and produce materially different basin-scale results. Without external observations this does not establish which product is correct. It establishes that the result is method dependent, and it puts a lower bound on the uncertainty that can be inferred from the products themselves. This test does not depend on which solution is selected above.

5. Ranking unstable

What it measures. Whether a basin occupies the same position in a global ranking of depletion trends under the two products, compared in percentile points.

The cut. A shift of more than 20 percentile points.

What tripping it means. A basin that is a high-priority depletion basin under one product may fall into a substantially different priority band under the other. For applications that rank basins rather than report a storage estimate, this instability may matter more than the absolute difference between the two estimates. A ranking that changes substantially with the choice of downscaled product is not a robust basis for prioritization.

What the tests cannot do

These tests produce red flags, not certificates of validity. A red flag means there is a specific reason not to treat a result as independently resolved or robust. An unflagged basin does not mean validated.

A basin that trips none of the tests has not been shown to be trustworthy. It survived a set of tests designed to identify specific, detectable failure modes. That is the full claim.

The tests cannot establish that a downscaled value is correct at fine spatial scales. They cannot establish that fine-scale variation represents groundwater rather than total terrestrial water storage. They cannot establish that apparent skill in a wet region reflects real spatial information rather than a shared response to precipitation.

Those questions require independent observations: wells, evapotranspiration, InSAR, or other measurements capable of testing the relevant mechanism. They also require an ablation test comparing predictor-only against predictor-plus-GRACE models against those observations. That is a separate validation exercise, and it is most informative where precipitation flux does not dominate the water balance.

The data

  • GRACE-SeDA v1, Gou and Soja, 0.5 degrees, 2002-04 to 2022-12, millimeters, baseline 2004 to 2009 removed. doi.org/10.3929/ethz-b-000648738. Paper: doi.org/10.1038/s44221-024-00194-w. Code: github.com/FoDcn/GRACE-SeDA.
  • Li and Kusche v2.0, 0.25 degrees, 2002-04 to 2025-03, stored in centimeters and converted to millimeters here. doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17265162. Paper: doi.org/10.1029/2025GL119881.
  • JPL mascon RL06.3Mv04 CRI, the coarse reference for Li and Kusche, whose file names no release. podaac.jpl.nasa.gov, TELLUS_GRAC-GRFO_MASCON_CRI_GRID_RL06.3_V4. Gain factors are deliberately not applied: they are a model-derived correction, and applying one would add the model structure this test looks for.
  • JPL mascon RL06.1Mv03 CRI, the release GRACE-SeDA v1 names as its input, and the coarse reference it is differenced against here. The collection is retired from the data catalogue; the file is still served at podaac-ops-cumulus-protected/TELLUS_GRAC-GRFO_MASCON_CRI_GRID_RL06.1_V3/. Differencing GRACE-SeDA against RL06.3 instead would put a release change into its residual and score it as information the downscaling added.
  • GSFC mascon RL06v2.0, a second coarse solution, used to measure how far apart two processing centers already are. earth.gsfc.nasa.gov/geo/data/grace-mascons.
  • HydroBASINS v1c level 6, 16,397 basins. hydrosheds.org/products/hydrobasins.
  • GLDAS 2.1 Noah 0.25 degrees, soil moisture, snow and canopy, and the precipitation forcing above 50 degrees. disc.gsfc.nasa.gov, GLDAS_NOAH025_M 2.1.
  • CHIRPS v2.0 monthly precipitation, 50S to 50N. data.chc.ucsb.edu, CHIRPS-2.0 global monthly.

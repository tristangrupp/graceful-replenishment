Distribution

How a basin gets its numbers

Boundaries are rasterized once at 0.05 degrees and each coarser grid inherits that overlap. A basin mean is the area-weighted mean of the cells over it, a conservative remapping. Missing cells leave both the numerator and the denominator, so a basin is renormalized over the area actually observed. Each product is scored against the coarse release it names as its input.

The five tests

testwhat it askscut
Spatial resolution
Not resolved		 Is the basin smaller than 2 product cells, or swallowed by a single mascon? Then the fine pattern comes from a background model, not from the satellite. under 2 cells, or over 95%
Signal contribution
No independent departure		 Does the product differ from the coarse data at all? Below the cut the downscaling added no basin-level information. under 5% of variance
Weather echoes
Weather field		 Is that departure an echo of the meteorology used to downscale it? Some relationship is expected, so read it as a degree. R² over 0.8
Method dependency
Products disagree		 Do the two products describe the same basin? r under 0.5, or trend gap over 1
Method dependency
Ranking unstable		 Does a basin's depletion rank hold between them? Beyond the cut a priority list would reorder. over 20 percentile points

The 5 percent cut is not arbitrary. Several labs turn the same satellite data into storage maps and their maps differ, which sets the size a departure has to beat. One lab across one version, JPL RL06.1 to RL06.3, changes 0.0005 of the coarse variance in the median basin. Changing lab, JPL to NASA Goddard, changes 0.25.

Disagreement does not say which product is right. It puts a floor under their combined error. Naming the right one needs wells, evapotranspiration or InSAR, which is the next tab and a different experiment.

Reference points

The data

  • GRACE-SeDA v1, Gou and Soja, 0.5 degrees, 2002-04 to 2022-12, millimeters, baseline 2004 to 2009 removed. doi.org/10.3929/ethz-b-000648738. Paper: doi.org/10.1038/s44221-024-00194-w. Code: github.com/FoDcn/GRACE-SeDA.
  • Li and Kusche v2.0, 0.25 degrees, 2002-04 to 2025-03, stored in centimeters and converted to millimeters here. doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17265162. Paper: doi.org/10.1029/2025GL119881.
  • JPL mascon RL06.3Mv04 CRI, the coarse reference for Li and Kusche, whose file names no release. podaac.jpl.nasa.gov, TELLUS_GRAC-GRFO_MASCON_CRI_GRID_RL06.3_V4. Gain factors are not applied: they are a model-derived correction, and applying one would add the model structure this test looks for.
  • JPL mascon RL06.1Mv03 CRI, the release GRACE-SeDA v1 names as its input, and the coarse reference used here. The collection is retired from the data catalogue; the file is still served at podaac-ops-cumulus-protected/TELLUS_GRAC-GRFO_MASCON_CRI_GRID_RL06.1_V3/. Differencing GRACE-SeDA against RL06.3 instead would put a release change into its residual and score it as information the downscaling added.
  • GSFC mascon RL06v2.0, a second coarse solution, used to measure how far apart two labs solving the same satellite data already are. earth.gsfc.nasa.gov/geo/data/grace-mascons.
  • HydroBASINS v1c level 6, 16,397 basins. hydrosheds.org/products/hydrobasins.
  • GLDAS 2.1 Noah 0.25 degrees, soil moisture, snow and canopy, and the precipitation forcing above 50 degrees. disc.gsfc.nasa.gov, GLDAS_NOAH025_M 2.1.
  • CHIRPS v2.0 monthly precipitation, 50S to 50N. data.chc.ucsb.edu, CHIRPS-2.0 global monthly.
  • Method note, tables and scripts: RED_METHODS.md and global/red in the repository.

Does downscaled GRACE add anything at level 6?

Two published global products claim to resolve water storage finer than GRACE measures it. GRACE-SeDA runs at 0.5 degrees; Li and Kusche runs at 0.25 degrees. Each one is tested on its own here, against the coarse JPL mascon solution that produced it, on 16,397 HydroBASINS level 6 basins, using no wells and no other outside data. Red means a basin failed at least one test. No basin turns green: passing means a basin survived tests built to catch obvious failure, which is not the same as being right.

How a basin gets its numbers

Each product sits on its own grid, so the basin outlines are rasterized once at 0.05 degrees and every coarser grid inherits its overlap from that raster. A basin mean is the sum of cell values times the area of each cell inside the basin, divided by the area inside the basin. That is what a conservative remap computes. Nearest neighbor and bilinear do not conserve mass and are used nowhere here. Cells missing in a month drop out of the numerator and the denominator together, so a partly covered basin is renormalized rather than scored as zero over the part that did not report.

Each product is then tested on its own. Nothing below asks the two products to agree, except the last two tests, which exist because their disagreement is measurable without outside data.

The five tests

Not resolved

What it measures. Whether the basin is large enough for the product to describe it as a field rather than a point, and whether GRACE ever measured anything inside it. Two numbers: how many product cells have their center in the basin, and what share of the basin's area falls in its single largest coarse mascon.

The cut. Fewer than 2 cells, or more than 95 percent of the area inside one mascon.

What tripping it means. A basin contained in one mascon has one gravimetric number attached to it, and one number has no shape. Whatever pattern a downscaled product draws across that basin came from the model field the downscaling was conditioned on, not from the satellites. That holds before any question of skill, and validation would not change it.

No departure

What it measures. How far the downscaled basin series moves away from the coarse basin series. Subtract the two monthly series and compare the variance of the difference with the variance of the coarse series.

The cut. Under 5 percent of the coarse variance.

What tripping it means. The product is the coarse series on a finer grid. That may be exactly what its authors intended, and nothing about it is wrong, but it holds nothing a user could not have read off the coarse solution. Read the number against the floor beneath it: two coarse solutions of the same months, from two processing centers, already differ by more than this cut in most basins.

Weather field

What it measures. Whether the departure is a hydrometeorological field wearing a gravimetry label. The departure series is regressed on basin precipitation at lags of zero to three months, the accumulated precipitation anomaly, soil moisture, and snow plus canopy.

The cut. Adjusted R squared above 0.8.

What tripping it means. Ranking basins by that departure ranks them by rainfall and soil moisture structure. Both products are built from GRACE plus model fields, so some of this follows by construction and a high value is not a scandal. Read it as a degree: a basin at 0.95 is a different object from one at 0.55. This test flags the fewest basins of the five, which is reported here rather than buried.

Products disagree

What it measures. Whether the two products describe the same basin. Their monthly series are correlated, and the gap between their fitted trends is compared with the size of the trend itself.

The cut. Correlation under 0.5, or a trend gap larger than the trend.

What tripping it means. Two methods built from the same measurements disagree, so at least one of them is wrong. That is a lower bound on error and it needs no wells to establish. This test does not depend on which solution is selected above.

Ranking unstable

What it measures. Where a basin sits in a global list ordered by depletion trend, under one product against the other, in percentile points.

The cut. A shift of more than 20 percentile points.

What tripping it means. A priority list built on one product puts this basin in a different band from a list built on the other. For any use that ranks basins rather than reporting a storage number, that is the failure that matters.

What the test cannot do

This produces red only. A basin that trips nothing has not been shown to be trustworthy. It survived tests built to catch obvious failure, and that is the whole claim.

It cannot say whether a downscaled value is correct anywhere. It cannot say whether fine structure reflects groundwater rather than total storage. It cannot say whether skill in a wet region is real or a shared response to rainfall. Each of those needs outside data, wells or evapotranspiration or InSAR, and an ablation of predictor-only against predictor-plus-GRACE scored against that data. That is a separate piece of work, and it reads cleanly only where precipitation flux does not dominate the water balance.

Reference points

The data

  • GRACE-SeDA v1, Gou and Soja, 0.5 degrees, 2002-04 to 2022-12, millimeters, baseline 2004 to 2009 removed. doi.org/10.3929/ethz-b-000648738. Paper: doi.org/10.1038/s44221-024-00194-w. Code: github.com/FoDcn/GRACE-SeDA.
  • Li and Kusche v2.0, 0.25 degrees, 2002-04 to 2025-03, stored in centimeters and converted to millimeters here. doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17265162. Paper: doi.org/10.1029/2025GL119881.
  • JPL mascon RL06.3Mv04 CRI, the coarse reference for Li and Kusche, whose own file names no release. podaac.jpl.nasa.gov, TELLUS_GRAC-GRFO_MASCON_CRI_GRID_RL06.3_V4. Gain factors are deliberately not applied: they are a model-derived correction, and applying one would add the model structure this test looks for.
  • JPL mascon RL06.1Mv03 CRI, the release GRACE-SeDA v1 names as its input, and the coarse reference it is differenced against here. The collection is retired from the data catalogue; the file is still served at podaac-ops-cumulus-protected/TELLUS_GRAC-GRFO_MASCON_CRI_GRID_RL06.1_V3/. Differencing GRACE-SeDA against RL06.3 instead would put a release change into its residual and score it as information the downscaling added. Moving between the two releases costs 0.0005 of the coarse variance in the median basin, against 0.25 for moving between two processing centers, so the correction matters for honesty more than for the answer.
  • GSFC mascon RL06v2.0, a second coarse solution, used to measure how far apart two processing centers already are. earth.gsfc.nasa.gov/geo/data/grace-mascons.
  • HydroBASINS v1c level 6, 16,397 basins. hydrosheds.org/products/hydrobasins.
  • GLDAS 2.1 Noah 0.25 degrees, soil moisture, snow and canopy, and the precipitation forcing above 50 degrees. disc.gsfc.nasa.gov, GLDAS_NOAH025_M 2.1.
  • CHIRPS v2.0 monthly precipitation, 50S to 50N. data.chc.ucsb.edu, CHIRPS-2.0 global monthly.
  • Method note, tables and scripts: RED_METHODS.md and global/red in the repository.
